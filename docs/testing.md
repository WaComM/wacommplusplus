# Testing

Run the dependency-light numerical suite with the commands in [build.md](build.md). It covers interpolation bounds, vertical storage mapping, physical-time weights, shortened forward/backward steps, irregular-time forward/backward restart clipping at an exact boundary, reflection, lossless worker partitioning, and deterministic counter-key random values. Application tests add exact integration of time-linear velocity including a shortened final step and an emission inside the interval, a deterministic forward/backward round trip, deterministic forward/backward continuation from a checkpoint inside a forcing interval, seeded stochastic repeatability, exact seeded continuation at a substep boundary, OpenMP thread-scheduling independence, concentration counts equal to a brute-force reference, exactly, on 1, 2, 3, 7, and 16 OpenMP threads with clustered, spread, dead, out-of-grid, and boundary particles added to existing counts, configuration round-trip and validation, versioned restart metadata, explicit particle-time slicing, 64-bit identities above `2^53`, and runtime-generated ROMS, native WACOMM, NEMO, HYCOM, WRF, and WW3 fixtures. Every ocean-adapter fixture also compares continuous and checkpoint-split deterministic integration in both directions. The environmental fixtures verify WRF grid-to-Earth wind rotation, WW3 aliases and longitude normalization, factory rejection, and dynamic wind/Stokes forward-backward composition. The fixtures collectively cover dimensions, masks, vertical orientation, normalized velocity, chronological time rejection, and cross-file boundary-record assembly.

`application_end_to_end` runs the complete `wacommplusplus` executable three times, forward from a GeoJSON point source, backward from the forward text endpoints, and forward with simulation start and release at `20210701Z0030`, on two generated native WACOMM windows carrying a uniform current. It requires the forward endpoint to match the analytic rhumb-line solution within 0.1 % of the 805 m path, the forward/backward round trip to close within 1 mm at 00 and 01 UTC, and every hourly history and gridded file to exist. The 00:30 run must release at 00:30, reach ages of 1800 s and 5400 s in the 01 and 02 UTC histories, and match the analytic endpoint for 5400 s of drift within the same 0.1 % tolerance. MPI builds launch both runs on two ranks, and OpenMP builds use two threads. It is the only test that executes the production `main`, and it found the output-cadence, text-precision, identity-parsing, and empty-JSON-restart defects documented in [application verification](application-verification.md). Its source is released at 10 m depth, and the test requires $k=-0.2$ within $10^{-12}$ at the forward end and at the backward origin. `vertical_coordinate_round_trip` checks that `deplatlon2kji` and `kji2deplatlon` are mutual inverses on a stretched five-level grid with sloping bathymetry: within $10^{-9}$ m in depth and $10^{-12}$ in $k$ on surface, interior, W-level, and lowest-valid-level positions. It also checks clamping below that level and rejection of non-finite depths ([vertical coordinate mapping](model.md#vertical-coordinate-mapping)). On a rotated, sheared curvilinear grid, it requires the horizontal round trip to close within $10^{-9}$ grid indices. Southern and western node lines must be accepted, and northern and eastern node lines, outside positions, and non-finite positions rejected ([horizontal position mapping](model.md#horizontal-position-mapping)). `source_position_loading` loads GeoJSON sources against a fixture grid. It accepts the `depth` property, the `dep` alias, and a third `Point` coordinate, and requires them to agree in magnitude. It checks the default surface depth and that a depth sets `k` at explicit indices. It rejects missing, `Polygon`, malformed, partial, and out-of-domain positions. It also requires a `saveAsJson`/`loadFromJson` round trip of a subsurface source within $10^{-9}$ in index and depth ([source position](configuration.md#source-position)). `polygon_release` samples a concave polygon with a hole on a fixture grid. It requires 2,000 particles with consecutive identities, every one inside the polygon at the release depth when mapped back by `kji2deplatlon`, and the indices that `deplatlon2kji` assigns. It requires the same cloud for the same seed, a different one for another seed, and a smaller release to be a prefix of a larger one. It requires the fraction south of 30°N in a 0–60°N box to be within five binomial standard deviations of $\sin30°/\sin60°$. It checks that each invalid geometry or property fails for its own reason, that out-of-domain and land-cell samples fail, that the cloud round-trips exactly through the version 3 restart, and that the oil-spill example polygons validate ([polygon releases](model.md#polygon-releases)). `application_end_to_end` also runs `createRestartFileFromJson` and the executable on polygon releases, forward and backward. `restart_format` additionally checks a bit-exact text round trip with an identity above $2^{53}$ and rejection of GeoJSON restarts. `output_writer` checks the [output publication](io.md) contract: byte-identical synchronous and asynchronous files, no remaining `.partial` file, a bounded queue that blocks and drains, failures that publish nothing and are reported once, staging, NetCDF-mutex serialization, Lustre `lfs setstripe` arguments and failure through a recording stand-in, and validation and round trip of the `io` publication keys. `application_end_to_end` additionally runs the executable forward and backward with asynchronous, staged, fsync publication and requires its outputs to equal the synchronous ones exactly. In builds with MPI and parallel NetCDF, `parallel_output_1_ranks`, `parallel_output_2_ranks`, and `parallel_output_3_ranks` compare the collective MPI-IO and parallel NetCDF-4 writers with the serial writers on 1–3 ranks, and `application_end_to_end` also runs the executable collectively on two ranks, forward and backward ([collective writes](io.md#collective-writes)). CI configures such a build with `WACOMM_BOOTSTRAP_PARALLEL_IO=ON` on Ubuntu and macOS and fails if the collective tests are not registered. `configuration_round_trip` rejects zero `restart.interval` and `io.timestep`, and checks that `physics.integrator` defaults to `midpoint`, round-trips `euler`, and rejects unknown values. `particle_physical_interval` measures the step-halving error ratio on a steady sheared current with an exponential analytic solution (Euler between 1.8 and 2.2, midpoint between 3.6 and 4.4), and checks midpoint backward return, restart continuation, and exact Euler fallback when the midpoint leaves the domain. `cuda_particle_parity` runs every case under both integrators.

The dependency-light `resource_limits` regression checks the start-up resource guards of [parallelism](parallelism.md#resource-limits). A mocked count of $2^{31}$ and of $2.16\times10^9$ particles must stop with an error naming `INT_MAX`, and $2^{31}-1$ must pass. The forcing estimate must equal, byte for byte, the arrays allocated with the adapters' shapes for one to three records, and 4,432,821,192 bytes for the Sarno window. The cgroup v2 and v1 limits and `MemAvailable` are parsed from generated directory trees, and the 80% and 100% thresholds are checked at their boundaries. It verifies arithmetic and parsing, not the memory of a real run.

The dependency-light `array_storage` regression constructs a non-owning five-dimensional view over caller storage, checks its row-major offset equation, mutates through the view, and verifies that stream traversal visits exactly the declared number of elements. This specifically protects the pointer/view boundary used by the environmental array layer; it is memory-layout verification, not numerical validation of a forcing field.

Application and backend validation additionally requires NetCDF fixtures and the relevant OpenMP, MPI, or CUDA runtime. Compare particle count, identity, position, health, and age with declared absolute and relative tolerances. Platform CI is build evidence, not proof of scientific equivalence.

`cuda_particle_parity` exercises nonuniform WRF wind and WW3 Stokes values at both physical-time records and all four cell corners. It compares the resulting complete CPU/CUDA particle state for forward, backward, and backward restart-clipped intervals, so the regression covers host-to-device transfer, common-grid bilinear interpolation, midpoint time interpolation, current-plus-leeway-plus-Stokes composition, direction-neutral environmental sampling, and continuation from a physical checkpoint. The executable reports a skip when no CUDA device is present; compile-only success is not measured parity.

The six-hour Sarno application on that node exposed a comparator defect rather than a model difference. Every CPU/GPU particle set had identical identities, counts, ages, times, and alive/dead states, and positions stayed within the declared limits, but about 3% of living particles differed in health \(h=\exp(-a/\tau_0)\) by one unit in the last place (\(1.1\times10^{-16}\)). Both backends evaluate the same expression from identical age \(a\) and decay time \(\tau_0\) (seconds); glibc and CUDA `exp` each guarantee at most about one unit in the last place, not mutual bitwise agreement. `tools/performance_evidence.py` previously required exact health, which held on the earlier V100 platform but is not a portable property. GPU comparisons now require exact alive/dead status and dead-particle values and bound living-particle health by the dimensionless absolute tolerance \(10^{-15}\); `sarno_protocol_collect` checks that a one-ulp difference passes and that \(10^{-6}\) or a survival-state change fails. CPU comparisons remain exact.

`sarno_core_sweep` tests the physical-core sweep collector on a synthetic suite: the median, speedup, efficiency, and GPU-ratio estimators, and rejection of two threads on one physical core, a sample validated against a different reference, and a wrong runtime thread count.

`sarno_backend_matrix` tests the backend-matrix collector on a synthetic suite: speedup and efficiency over $p\cdot n$ workers, best-tuple selection by solver and application time, refusal of resource tuples a build cannot provide, the bound and unbound `mpirun` command lines, even multi-node `ppr` placement and rejection of an uneven one, summation of the rank-0 `DISTRIBUTE`/`COLLECT` times over the five intervals and of the `FORCING` (rank 0 and slowest rank), `SETUP`, `OUTPUT`, `FLUSH`, and `RUN` phase times over the six windows, including the forcing parts (lock wait, read, normalization, boundary) and zero parts for the earlier forcing line, the `numactl` launch prefix outside and inside `mpirun`, refusal to resume a suite with other `--io-async` or `--interleave` settings, and rejection of a wrong runtime rank count, a different reference, and a missing CUDA device. Results are in the [backend matrix evaluation](backend-matrix-evaluation.md).

The full six-hour Sarno application was rerun on `gn03` in CPU Slurm job 6360 and CUDA job 6361, using one MPI rank and one OpenMP thread per run with identical configuration and source hashes. Both jobs exited zero. All five saved particle sets have identical IDs, counts, discrete states, ages, and physical times. The maximum spherical horizontal separation is $2.19\times10^{-7}$ m, maximum depth difference is $2.25\times10^{-9}$ m, and maximum dimensionless grid-index difference is $2.66\times10^{-9}$, below stated numerical-equivalence limits of $10^{-6}$ m, $10^{-8}$ m, and $10^{-8}$ respectively. Every variable in the five gridded outputs agrees exactly. The exact snapshot comparator fails on floating-point depth at the first time, as expected for this tolerance-based comparison. The [per-time report](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/figures/gn03-cpu-gpu-consistency.json) and [example discussion](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/README.md#mpi-openmp-and-cuda-performance-diagnostic-on-low-gn) give provenance and limits; this is numerical backend verification, not observational validation.

When configured with `USE_MPI=ON`, CTest launches a two-rank decomposition test over eleven particles. It uses the same committed MPI datatype as the application, performs the seeded stochastic particle update after a non-uniform scatter, gathers the state, and compares identity, position, health, age, and emission time exactly with serial execution. Two ranks fit the minimum GitHub-hosted runner allocation without implementation-specific oversubscription flags, while the odd particle count still exercises a remainder on rank zero.

With `USE_OMP=ON`, the same test performs local particle updates under dynamic OpenMP scheduling. Additional CTest cases use one rank with one thread and two ranks with two threads per rank; both compare every gathered particle against a serial reference. These are numerical backend-equivalence checks for the synthetic seeded interval, not observational validation. The CUDA case remains a separate CPU/device particle-state comparison and must execute on a physical GPU before CUDA parity can be claimed.

Test targets explicitly undefine `NDEBUG`, including in Release configurations, because the regression executables use C assertions as their failure mechanism. A Release CI pass therefore executes the numerical and metadata checks rather than only launching empty test bodies.

When Python 3 is available, CTest also creates a temporary reproducibility manifest and verifies ocean, weather, wave, and output SHA-256 values, selected CMake options, declared tolerances, and schema version. Python is optional and is not required to configure or build the portable C++17 numerical core.

When the optional Python `netCDF4` package is available, `trajectory_diagnostics` creates deliberately unordered synthetic snapshots, filters an inactive member, joins stable identities, preserves embedded revision provenance, handles an antimeridian-crossing ensemble, verifies physical-time ordering and nonzero spread, and inspects required SVG metadata. CTest reports a skip when that optional analysis dependency is absent; this does not affect the C++ model build.

The environmental-adapter fixture additionally checks WRF timestamp parsing and grid-vector rotation, CF time conversion from minutes to the WaComM epoch, CF-oriented Stokes aliases, longitude normalization, and rejection of unsupported velocity units. The documentation-visual test parses every repository SVG, requires accessible title and description elements, verifies that each asset is referenced by a document, and checks that the OpenDrift comparison contains a displayed governing equation and explicitly identifies its conceptual map as non-georeferenced. This is structural verification; scientific review remains responsible for the truth and interpretability of each visual.

The dependency-free `native_hardware_validation_contract` test keeps both self-hosted runner label sets, native preflight invocations, complete CTest and dynamic-link gates, evidence upload, device-tree and emulation rejection, and paired operator guides connected. It is a structural regression test; only execution of the dedicated workflow on a physical board supplies native-hardware evidence.

Remote transport has a separate network integration test backed by the official OPeNDAP test service. Configure with `-DBUILD_REMOTE_INTEGRATION_TESTS=ON`, build, and run `ctest --test-dir build -R remote_netcdf_integration --output-on-failure`. The test opens the dataset through NetCDF, verifies its remote dimensions, and reads coordinate and data hyperslabs. It intentionally remains outside the default offline suite because service or network failure is distinct from a WaComM++ regression. Application configuration itself fails unless the linked NetCDF headers declare both DAP2 and DAP4 support.

The dependency-light `environmental_regridding` test verifies exact preservation of a constant field, affine-field interpolation on a refined target grid, increasing and decreasing axes, antimeridian crossing, inverse mapping within a warped curvilinear cell, indexed lookup across a 600-cell analytically representable warped grid, and rejection of extrapolation, a non-rectilinear source passed to the rectilinear operator, and folded geometry. Application adapter tests additionally exercise regridding through the public wave-adapter interface. These are verification tests of the implemented operator, not evidence that bilinear interpolation is scientifically suitable for every forcing product or a performance guarantee for all grid geometries.

With `USE_PROJ=ON`, tests check EPSG:4326-to-EPSG:3857 control coordinates, affine rectilinear and warped curvilinear fields, metric `x/y` parsing for WRF and WW3, indexed Cartesian location, and preservation of Earth-relative components. The default build verifies fail-fast rejection. These comparisons verify implementation behavior, not every CRS or datum resource.

The conservative-remapping tests verify exact rectilinear spherical overlaps, convex curvilinear polygon clipping in equal-area coordinates, constants, area-weighted aggregation, antimeridian handling, fractional active area, a linear second-order reconstruction, equality of first/second-order common-domain integrals, unchanged time-record ordering, and rejection of incomplete, overlapping, non-rectilinear, or folded geometry. These are operator-verification identities, not validation of a particular geophysical scalar or authorization to remap environmental velocity components conservatively.

`.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs on every branch push, pull request, and manual dispatch. Its `workflow-lint` job checks every workflow with actionlint 1.7.12, including shellcheck of `run` scripts, and `.github/actionlint.yaml` declares the self-hosted runner labels. It runs portable platform checks plus complete serial application builds on Ubuntu, Rocky Linux 8.9, macOS Intel, macOS Apple Silicon, Windows/MSVC with vcpkg, and a Raspberry Pi OS ARM64 proxy. Ubuntu additionally validates OpenMP, MPI, combined MPI/OpenMP, and `USE_PROJ=ON` builds. macOS Apple Silicon adds a combined OpenMP/MPI/PROJ build. The Ubuntu and macOS application jobs install the pinned `tools/requirements-figures.txt` packages under Python 3.11, pass that interpreter to CMake, and fail if any CTest case reports a skip, so the Python diagnostic regressions actually run. On failure they upload the CTest logs and the `application-end-to-end` working directory as artifacts; the Rocky Linux and Windows jobs upload their CTest logs, and Windows also uploads the private-dependency configure logs. `.github/workflows/hpc.yml` compiles the combined MPI/OpenMP/CUDA hierarchy, tests OpenACC, and builds the complete application under Debian Trixie riscv64 through QEMU. `.github/workflows/native-hardware.yml` manually runs the complete serial configure, build, CTest, install, and dynamic-link gates on directly hosted Raspberry Pi ARM64 and RV64 runners. Its preflight rejects the wrong kernel architecture, a mismatched or QEMU device-tree identity, and container execution, then archives reproducibility evidence even when a later gate fails. The CUDA kernel follows the CPU physical-time interval, interpolation, direction, closure, grid-metric, vertical-stride, and counter-key stochastic rules. `cuda_particle_parity` compares deterministic and seeded-stochastic CPU/GPU state when a device is present and reports a CTest skip when it is not. `.github/workflows/gpu.yml` executes that test on a manually selected self-hosted GPU runner, while `.github/workflows/flexmpi.yml` validates a self-hosted FlexMPI installation. Tagged releases call the complete `ci.yml` matrix as a reusable workflow at the tagged revision and also require serial, OpenACC, and CUDA compile/CPU-test validation before creating a deterministic source archive and SHA-256 checksum. The release also publishes a build-metadata file (Git revision, workflow run, runner, compiler, CMake version and options, NetCDF, log4cplus, and nlohmann-json versions, source and installed-executable SHA-256) and the list of registered CTest cases. `.github/dependabot.yml` opens weekly grouped pull requests for GitHub Actions updates, which pass through the same CI.

All workflows declare the minimum repository permission they require and bound job duration. Container package installation is noninteractive. Native hardware runs are not cancelled automatically so that an evidence bundle is not discarded by a second dispatch. A successful CUDA container build with a skipped parity test remains compile-only evidence; a successful self-hosted GPU workflow is required before a release can claim measured CUDA equivalence. FlexMPI claims likewise require the dedicated workflow on an installation providing `empi.h`, the EMPI library, and its configured MPI runtime. Raspberry Pi or RV64 native claims require an archived artifact from the dedicated workflow at the claimed Git revision; hosted ARM64, cross-compile, and QEMU results are insufficient.

Surface-drift validation adds zero-wind finiteness, pure downwind direction, equal-and-opposite symmetric crosswind motion, explicitly asymmetric left/right regression selection, catalog identity and provenance, process-catalog metadata, configuration validation, deterministic forward/backward current-plus-leeway reversal, version 3 restart persistence, MPI metadata preservation, and deterministic CPU/CUDA leeway parity. Existing object IDs are unchanged. Coefficient-ensemble tests verify the residual equation, correlation transform, same-seed/same-identity repeatability, distinct members, configuration persistence, and CPU/CUDA parity. Wind-error tests verify disjoint component keys, distinct identities, exact continuation at a completed substep, validation, JSON persistence, and CUDA parity for constant wind. Random-side and jibing tests cover threshold boundaries, disjoint streams, hazard conversion, limiting probabilities, forced-transition state, and continuous/restarted forward and backward execution. These are numerical verification results, not observational calibration; CUDA assertions execute only on a capable host.

Correlated forcing-error verification additionally covers shared spatial/physical-time bin keys, distinct-bin draws, component-covariance bounds, configuration round trips, zero-scale legacy behavior, and physical-time restart keying. CUDA parity uses the same binning and Cholesky equations when a capable device is available.

Regional-calibration verification covers geographic inside/outside selection, mandatory provenance, matching object and forcing identities, finite ordered EPSG:4326 bounds, non-overlap, positive standard deviation, PSD correlation, nonnegative support scales, checksum syntax, sample size, resolved-configuration round trips, and the shared CPU/CUDA selector. These tests verify implementation and backend equivalence; they do not validate any external observational estimate.

Private Unix OpenSSL builds add `openssl_dependency`, which requires exact agreement between the pinned version, compiled headers, linked OpenSSL, and curl's reported TLS backend, and creates a client TLS context. It is an offline dependency-integration check; it does not test remote servers or certificate trust. The portable core does not require OpenSSL, Perl, or make through this dependency path.

`source_emission` verifies exact hourly counts, midpoint times, multi-hour forcing gaps, partial-interval decomposition, bounded active windows, pulse emission, legacy batches, invalid discrete counts, and seeded source-position key separation. The common scheduler runs before MPI/OpenMP/CUDA distribution, while particle parity tests verify the shared stored-time activation rule.

`publication_figures` is an optional Python regression requiring the plotting requirements. It checks analytical bin counts and quantiles, signed-depth conversion, masked/inactive selection, exact large integer IDs, duplicate time/ID and invalid unit rejection, deterministic rendering under input permutation, accessible exports, and read-only inputs. A CTest skip means plotting dependencies are absent, not that rendering was verified. The six-hour Slurm run is model execution evidence; its source scheduling and missing forcing hour are documented in its [guide](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/README.md).

The GNU 12.2.1/OpenMPI 4.1.4 build passed all 22 CTest checks without skips; the [validation log](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/figures/mpi2-validation.log) includes MPI decomposition, restart, adapters, numerical tests, documentation, and figures.

`particle_snapshot_comparison` requires NumPy and NetCDF4 (skip code 77 when absent). It verifies read-only input handling, row-order independence with exact IDs above 2^53, and rejection of altered values, duplicate identifiers, and mismatched physical times. The [MPI Sarno report](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/figures/mpi2-comparison.json) additionally verifies all stored particle variables at five physical times against serial execution with zero numeric tolerance. Global build attributes are excluded; gridded counts and normalized forcing are not compared by this test.

`scaling_figures` uses the optional plotting dependencies and skips with code 77 when absent. Its analytical tests verify process-order independence, ideal speedup/efficiency, nonideal scaling, rejection of nonpositive/non-finite times and invalid process/thread counts, native-forcing configuration validation, rejection of missing/duplicate/reordered or non-finite solver intervals, and accessible SVG/PDF/PNG exports. The six actual [Sarno scaling runs](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/README.md#mpiopenmp-strong-scaling) additionally require exact particle-state agreement before figures can be generated. The timing instrument uses pre/post MPI barriers and a monotonic clock; the plotted interval excludes forcing work and file writes. No model equations or numerical tolerances change.

The native-adapter regression reads a real fixture through both `WACOMM` and historical `WaComM` factory selections, and covers saved windows that already include an adjacent endpoint. It requires unchanged record count for forward/backward reuse, rejects differences in elevation, horizontal velocities, vertical velocity, and diffusivity independently, and reruns the forward/backward restart-equivalence assertions on the unchanged window. The shared loader handles serial and MPI/OpenMP execution; no backend-specific equations are added.

The final native-replay/timing change passed all [22 serial application checks](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/figures/scaling/validation-serial.log), [23 MPI/OpenMP checks](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/figures/scaling/validation-mpi-openmp.log), and [12 portable-core/diagnostic checks](../examples/wacomm-sarno-lite/docs/figures/scaling/validation-core.log), without skips. `openmp_scaling_figures` checks exact thread-count coverage, one-process enforcement, ideal and nonideal speedup/efficiency, input-order independence, rejection of invalid times, and accessible SVG/PDF/PNG exports.
